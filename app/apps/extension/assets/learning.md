We remember what the learner has shown, what they might be unsure about,
and what we have not checked. These notes guide the next question; they are
not a grade or a permanent judgment about ability.

## Learner

- Name: Demo learner
- Current topic: Photosynthesis
- Goal: Explain how plants use light to make glucose
- Preferred explanation: A short diagram, then a question

## What seems understood

- Plants need light for photosynthesis.
  Evidence: "Without light, the plant cannot keep making glucose."
  Confidence: Tentative — one correct answer; check again in a new example.

- Glucose is something the plant makes.
  Evidence: The learner labelled glucose as an output on their whiteboard.
  Confidence: Tentative — the label was added after a hint.

## What might need another check

- Light energy versus food.
  Evidence: The learner first said, "Sunlight is the plant's food."
  After a diagram, they changed this to "Sunlight gives it energy."
  Current note: May understand the distinction now; an independent
  explanation is still needed before treating the gap as resolved.

- Where the carbon in glucose comes from.
  Evidence: The learner was unsure whether it came from water or the air.
  Current note: Revisit carbon dioxide, then ask them to trace the inputs.

## Not checked yet

- What chlorophyll does
- Why light alone is not enough for photosynthesis
- How photosynthesis differs from respiration

Unknown means we have no evidence yet. Opening notes, spending time on a
page, skipping a question, or staying quiet does not show understanding
or a lack of it.

## Next teaching move

1. Ask: "Is sunlight an ingredient or a source of energy? Why?"
2. Use a labelled inputs → leaf → outputs diagram if the answer is unsure.
3. Ask a fresh question without hints before updating the observation.
4. Keep unresolved questions visible for the next session.

## When these notes change

- Add the learner's actual answer or drawing as evidence.
- Distinguish an independent answer from one given after a hint.
- Revise earlier impressions when new evidence disagrees.
- Let the learner inspect and correct what has been recorded.

This bundled example is static. It does not add observations to a profile
or change a lesson. Real observations are shown under **My learning**.
