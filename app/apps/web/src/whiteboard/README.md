# Lesson whiteboard

Open a lesson and click **Whiteboard**, the first tab above the source documents. It stays visible while you scroll through document tabs; its tooltip reads **Draw with Excalidraw**. On narrow screens the canvas retains a 420 px drawing area.

The lesson Whiteboard tab embeds Excalidraw, bundled locally for the web app and Chrome extension. Students can draw, edit shapes and arrows, add text and images, undo/redo, and import/export Excalidraw files using its native controls.

**Teach me at the whiteboard.** This lesson action opens the board and runs the whole lesson there. Every turn sent while the Whiteboard tab is open carries `whiteboard: true`, and the tutor receives its own current items (`tutorBoard`) so it can grow one diagram across turns: unchanged items come back with the same id and are kept even when reordered, changed ones are redrawn, omitted ones disappear. New items are revealed one at a time (about 0.4 s apart, skipped when the OS asks for reduced motion). The viewport frames the complete diagram once so it stays steady while drawing. **Fit drawing** brings the whole scene back into view. Up to 40 items per reply.

Tutor diagrams use crisp green outlines, pale filled shapes, 20 px shape labels and 16 px connector labels. Notes wrap instead of running across the canvas. The tutor is instructed to use aligned process, comparison, hierarchy or cycle layouts with short labels and space for connectors; detailed explanations stay in the conversation. An arrow starts at `(x, y)` and ends at `(x + width, y + height)`, with signed displacements supporting leftward and upward arrows. Rectangle, ellipse and text dimensions remain nonnegative. Geometry and label rendering are tested deterministically; a model's choice of diagram and its placement remain generated output.

**Ask about your drawing.** The field above the canvas sends a question to the tutor together with a JPEG snapshot of the board (Excalidraw's own export, at most 1400 px, made in the browser) and a list of the student's shapes (id, type, position, size, text) that the API derives from the saved scene. The reply's board items may name a shape as `target`: text and arrows point at it, rectangles and ellipses ring it. Every tutor mark uses one green stroke and carries `customData.boardItemId`, so the next annotated reply replaces the previous marks while student shapes stay untouched; marks are ordinary elements the student can move or delete. Questions asked from a document tab work the same way without the picture, and the answer then offers **See it on the whiteboard**. The response schema restricts targets to the student shape IDs supplied for the current turn (or null); tutor-created diagrams use coordinates with null targets, including on empty boards. The server also rejects a reply that points at a shape not on the board, and the tutor cannot move or delete student shapes.

Scenes autosave through the authenticated lesson board endpoint; Save retries failures and reports the server's error. Only one save runs at a time; edits made during it are combined into the latest scene for the next save, instead of queuing every intermediate drawing. Save, changing tabs, and sending another turn finish any active reveal before saving, retaining the student's edits and the complete diagram. Reduced-motion diagrams also autosave on opening. Teaching and voice wait for pending saves. Existing notes and strokes migrate on opening. Changed tutor diagrams replace their previous shapes while unrelated student drawings remain. **Add text** places a thought below existing content and brings it into view, so successive notes do not overlap or disappear offscreen.

The optional `Board.scene` stores elements (including deletion markers), embedded image files, and the source items used to reconcile tutor updates. Existing `items` and `strokes` remain compatible with older lessons. Pan, zoom, selection and undo history are session-only. No multiplayer service is configured.

Board requests are capped at 10 MB, 2,000 scene elements and 100 embedded images. Turn requests carrying a board snapshot are capped at 3 MB, with the snapshot itself at most 2,000,000 characters of PNG, JPEG or WebP data URL; the tutor sees at most 120 student shapes. Invalid geometry, duplicate IDs, external image URLs, unsafe links and embedded webpages are rejected. Failed saves retain the current draft; reduce oversized content and retry before closing the lesson.

Run `cd app && bun run check && bun run test:e2e`. `scripts/build.ts` bundles the editor and copies its fonts to both distribution folders; no drawing-site account is required.
